# Resident server steward · `proposal:residentsteward:resident-server-steward`

Status: DRAFT — the programme requirements are fixed; the operating choices marked as proposals remain subject to the open questions at the end.

## Purpose and decision provenance · `sec:residentsteward:purpose-and-decision-provenance`

A root-full server should be governable from the server even when its usual workstation is absent. The programme therefore requires a standard resident administrative agent, a dedicated steward account class parallel to the human owner's class, an ownership-authenticated owner channel, stewardship of staff correspondence, policy-led resource arbitration, an explicit untrusted-input boundary, bounded liveness, outside transcript oversight, and owner-only credential provisioning. Those requirements are the premises of this document rather than choices reopened here.

This proposal supplies the mechanisms the programme leaves open: a timer-driven headless invocation rather than a perpetual model session, a one-consumer handoff rule, owner-folder letters paired with an operational journal, a cursored external transcript feed, durable case identifiers and cursors, and fail-loud service boundaries. These are proposal choices, and the alternatives that still need operational or owner judgment are listed explicitly under Open questions.

The steward extends the [root-server account model](root-server-account-model.md) by adding a standard account class alongside rather than inside the owner class, uses the measured privilege transition in [confined administration](../confined-admin.md), and inherits the event classification and cursor discipline of [correspondence-watch](../../infra/correspondence-watch/README.md). It does not replace the human owner or rescue class and does not relax confinement.

## The steward account class · `sec:residentsteward:the-steward-account-class`

The programme defines `steward` as a standard named account class on every root-full server, parallel to and distinct from the human-owned `owner` class. The account is mapped to `staff_u`, enrolled in wheel, and given the explicit `staff_r` to `sysadm_r` and `staff_t` to `sysadm_t` sudo transition. It is not uid 0, and the CLI, scheduler, model, tools, and ordinary child processes always run as `steward` in the confined domain; the steward never runs as `owner` or root.

That combination is stronger than either half by itself. Confinement limits the ambient reach of the CLI, its tools, and any material it reads; wheel provides the machine-local authority the role exists to exercise; the sysadm transition makes that authority usable on a confined host where uid 0 in `staff_t` still cannot administer system files. Administrative acts cross the boundary through `sudo`, so the journal attributes each privileged request to `steward` and the privileged domain exists only for that action. A permanently unconfined steward would turn every parser, prompt, and plugin reachable by the agent into root-adjacent attack surface, while a confined account without the transition would be an observer that must relay every useful act elsewhere.

The dedicated uid is also an attribution boundary. It separates steward files, credentials, process accounting, service logs, transcripts, and sudo records from the human owner and from every staff workload. Sharing `owner` would make a routine agent act indistinguishable from human administration, and running a root daemon would erase the deliberate transition that makes privilege reviewable. The fixed account name makes this boundary uniform across servers without making credentials or state portable between them.

The owner account remains the human's account, directly usable for standard provisioning, bootstrap, and human-directed recovery, and outside the steward's credential custody. Any separate human rescue account remains human-controlled as well. Routine administration moves to `steward` after handoff, preserving independent recovery paths without ever borrowing their identities for the agent.

## The owner correspondence channel · `sec:residentsteward:the-owner-correspondence-channel`

The programme makes the owner account's correspondence folder the workstation-to-steward control plane. The folder is owned by `owner` and is not group-writable or world-writable, so an ordinary file created there can originate only from the owner account or root. A steward accepts an owner-owned pending letter in that folder as an authenticated work order; a path, filename, or prose claim copied from a staff folder never acquires that status merely by referring to the owner.

File ownership authenticates the channel boundary, not the truth of every statement inside a letter. An owner work order may quote a staff request or attach hostile material, so the steward separates the authenticated directive and constraints from embedded evidence and evaluates the latter as data. Root ownership is reserved for steward reports and administrative delivery; those files are output, not fresh work orders, which prevents the steward from waking on its own replies.

The workstation orchestrator uses the owner-authorized transport only to deposit work orders, read dispositions, invoke the fixed read-only transcript exporter, and perform the bounded bootstrap needed to establish or recover the resident service. Once the handoff is accepted, it does not run routine sudo commands, answer staff correspondence in parallel, or arbitrate server resources directly. The retained direct administrative paths are human rescue and bootstrap: a human may enter through the owner or rescue account when the steward cannot operate, and the owner must establish the standard steward kit and provision its CLI credential before the steward can run; external transcript reading is oversight, not administration.

This withdrawal is a work-ownership rule as well as a transport choice. Possession of an owner SSH key may leave a technical route to a shell, but the normal orchestration contract makes a letter the only new-work boundary. Emergency direct administration is followed by a reconciliation letter naming what changed, so the steward's local view does not silently diverge from machine state.

## Duties · `sec:residentsteward:duties`

### Correspondence stewardship · `sec:residentsteward:correspondence-stewardship`

The programme transfers every staff correspondence folder from the workstation correspondence consumer to the resident steward's beat. For each new staff-owned letter the steward opens a case, identifies the request and the policy that governs it, answers in the same folder, performs any authorized follow-up, and escalates decisions outside its charter through the owner channel. Replies are installed as root-owned or conventionally named administrative replies so the scanner records them as context without classifying them as new user work.

Stewardship is not automatic approval. A standard request already authorized by the standing charter can be fulfilled and recorded; an ambiguous, novel, destructive, or authority-expanding request is summarized for the owner with options and a recommendation. The staff member receives a notice that the request is pending owner judgment, so escalation is visible at both ends rather than becoming a silent queue.

### The resource policy ladder · `sec:residentsteward:the-resource-policy-ladder`

The programme sets the following ordered policy. The order matters because it prefers attribution, notice, and reversible pressure before destructive relief.

1. **Measure and attribute.** Collect per-account and per-workload evidence, identify whether the pressure is transient or sustained, and record the observation before acting. Aggregate machine pressure without ownership data is not enough to choose a victim.
2. **Notify through correspondence.** Tell the affected account what pressure exists, what evidence associates its workload with the pressure, what action is proposed, and when it will occur. If the machine is already at risk, notice may accompany rather than precede the action, but it is never omitted.
3. **Reclaim idle RAM before active work.** An idle user does not own resident memory merely because its processes allocated it first. The steward first pressures idle sessions and caches toward swap while protecting active interactive and checkpointing work.
4. **Clear idle GPU allocations.** GPU memory held by a workload that is no longer doing useful work is released after the workload and its owner are identified. A long kernel, blocked data loader, or scheduled pause is not declared idle from utilization percentage alone.
5. **Collect disk-usage data before eviction.** A filling disk triggers an attributed inventory, growth-rate observation, and classification of disposable versus durable data before any removal. The steward asks the owning account to clean up when time permits, then evicts only under the standing retention policy or an owner-approved exception; fullness never licenses blind deletion.
6. **Evict idle workloads when swap is full.** Full swap removes the buffer that made gentler RAM pressure safe. The steward then stops the least valuable idle workloads needed to restore headroom, records why each victim was idle and preferable to alternatives, and reports the outcome to every affected account and the owner.

The ladder is not a fixed escalation timer. It expresses precedence and evidence duties: a disk event cannot skip measurement, a GPU event cannot infer idleness from one sample, and urgent memory pressure can compress the notice interval without erasing the notice. Resource action that destroys durable data, changes a quota, or displaces active work remains outside the ladder unless the standing charter or an owner work order grants it explicitly.

### Why arbitration requires an agent · `sec:residentsteward:why-arbitration-requires-an-agent`

Scripts can collect bytes, utilization, process age, terminal activity, cgroup pressure, and growth rates, but those values do not answer whether a workload is abandoned, awaiting a scarce dependency, preserving an expensive checkpoint, or serving a human who will return shortly. A single threshold either evicts legitimate work or leaves obvious waste untouched. The steward is an agent because it must combine measurements with correspondence, declared priorities, workload intent, reversibility, and the cost of being wrong, then explain the judgment in terms another operator can audit.

Automation still has a narrow role proposed here: sensors may open a resource case when pressure crosses a conservative observation threshold, and safe collectors may attach facts to it. They do not select victims or perform eviction. The model applies the ladder, and privileged action remains an explicit, journaled step.

## Security spine · `sec:residentsteward:security-spine`

Every staff letter is untrusted input. A request may contain shell text, links, patches, copied logs, claims about approvals, or instructions aimed at the model; none of those forms grants authority. The steward may inspect them as evidence, but it never treats them as commands to execute, policy to install, or permission to expand access.

The injection risk is structural rather than hypothetical: the steward is both a language model that interprets prose and an account capable of transitioning to administrative power. If prose in any scanned folder could redefine the task, a staff account could obtain root effects by writing persuasive text into its own home, bypassing Unix ownership, sudo attribution, and the account model. Treating the whole staff channel as data preserves those operating-system boundaries inside the model's reasoning boundary.

There are exactly two authority sources. The standing charter authorizes recurring classes of work, limits and escalation rules; an owner-owned work order in the ownership-authenticated owner folder authorizes a particular task or exception. Staff letters, machine output, repository content, web pages, tool results, attachments, steward transcripts, Sonnet watcher summaries, and quoted text are evidence only. When the sources conflict, the narrower owner work order may choose among options the charter permits but may not silently erase hard charter constraints; a requested charter change is escalated as such and installed through the owner-controlled process.

Before privileged execution, the steward restates the intended effect, target, authority source, reversibility, and notification state in its case record. It resolves identifiers from machine state instead of trusting letter-supplied paths or process ids, uses least-scope commands, and reads back the result. These checks are proposed operating controls derived from the programme's untrusted-input rule; they make the privilege boundary inspectable rather than relying on the model to remember that a letter was hostile.

## Liveness · `sec:residentsteward:liveness`

The programme rules out a perpetual model session that can park and requires the timer-driven shape to be weighed against a supervised long-lived session. A supervisor can restart a process that exits or crashes, but it cannot reliably detect a live model that has reasoned itself into waiting forever. A quiet long-lived session, a wedged input stream, and a correctly idle session can all look healthy at the process boundary; prompt instructions do not turn that ambiguity into liveness.

A timer-driven headless invocation makes waiting an operating-system responsibility instead. A small non-model wrapper checks a durable correspondence cursor and resource-case queue, takes a non-overlapping lock, invokes the steward only when work is pending, imposes an elapsed-wall deadline, and exits with a named quiet, success, or failure result. Each invocation starts from the standing charter plus the pending cases, writes dispositions before advancing their cursor, and terminates; if it crashes, the unadvanced case remains pending for the next tick.

The recommended design is therefore a system timer and bounded headless invocation, not a supervised perpetual session. The timer is persistent across reboot, the service runs as the confined steward account, overlapping runs are refused rather than accumulated, and failures are visible in both service state and the journal. A long-lived session offers lower startup cost and conversational continuity, but those savings do not compensate for an unobservable parked state on an administrative control plane; continuity belongs in durable cases and reports, not model memory.

The wrapper must distinguish silence from blindness. A healthy scan with no work is a named quiet result, inability to read a source or invoke the CLI is a nonzero failure, partial visibility is a warning, and every network or subprocess operation has a deadline. These requirements carry the correspondence consumer lessons into the resident service rather than merely relocating the old failure mode onto an always-on machine.

## Reporting line and race ownership · `sec:residentsteward:reporting-line-and-race-ownership`

The proposal uses both letters and a journal because they answer different questions. The steward installs a disposition letter in the owner correspondence folder for every owner work order, escalation, exceptional resource action, and service degradation; that is the conversational record the workstation and human owner can read and answer. The system journal records invocation start and finish, cursor movement, case identifiers, sudo action summaries, warnings, failures, and duration; that is the machine-health and audit stream operators can query without parsing prose files.

A letter without journal telemetry can say what the steward intended while concealing that its scanner has been blind for hours. A journal without letters makes decisions hard to review, acknowledge, or continue as a conversation. Pairing them keeps policy decisions durable at the control-plane boundary and liveness facts in the system designed to supervise processes.

Every work order receives a stable case identifier, and every disposition names it. The workstation owns cross-server programmes, repository work, and the act of issuing or acknowledging owner work orders; the steward owns server-local correspondence, resource cases, and administrative execution after acceptance. Neither actor performs the other's in-flight case: the workstation deposits once and assigns observation of the disposition and its deadline to the relevant lane's Sonnet watcher, while the steward claims once and reports completion, refusal, or escalation before moving the durable cursor.

The handoff has one consumer at a time. The workstation correspondence consumer is drained and stopped before the steward consumer is enabled, a named cursor boundary is recorded, and the first post-boundary owner work order is used as the acceptance probe. During steward outage the workstation may observe journals and letters, but it does not quietly resume ordinary administration; a human explicitly declares rescue mode, performs the minimum recovery, and leaves a reconciliation letter before normal ownership returns to the steward.

## External transcript oversight · `sec:residentsteward:external-transcript-oversight`

The programme requires the steward's judgment to be watched from outside the machine it administers. A wheel-carrying agent that produces and interprets all of its own evidence would concentrate authority, observation, and explanation in one failure domain; correspondence letters and local journal entries are necessary records, but the administrator can affect the machine that stores them. The relevant oversight lane's Sonnet watcher gives the human owner and workstation orchestrator an independent view of what the steward considered and did.

The source is the steward account's own Claude session-transcript tree under its home, including the primary session records and any nested agent-session records produced beneath the CLI's projects directory. A per-server capture tool reads only appended transcript bytes through a bounded read path, keeps file-identity and offset cursors on the workstation, detects truncation or replacement, and prefixes every emitted record with its server and relative transcript path. Every server has a separate cursor and no server inherits another's continuity assumption.

For orchestration-side observation, the oversight lane's Sonnet watcher re-invokes the bounded capture tool in foreground calls and owns the waiting, relay and deadline under (`proto:dispatch:log-eater-callback`); the session runs no supervisor watching script. Total read failure is loud and nonzero, partial visibility is a named warning, aliases are validated before polling, and each remote read and overall call has an elapsed-wall deadline. Cursor state advances only for successfully read files; retained program names and cursors are unchanged by this division of duties.

Noise filtering decides when to wake the Sonnet watcher, not what the source said. The capture tool may suppress duplicate stream fragments, routine progress frames, and bulk tool payload already represented by a command result, but it preserves owner and staff prompts, assistant decisions, case identifiers, commands, sudo transitions, refusals, errors, warnings, and evidence of transcript truncation; every filtered batch reports counts so a filter cannot silently become an audit gap. Filters are pinned against fixtures before deployment and changed only as reviewed workstation code.

Transcript content crosses the server-to-workstation trust boundary as data. It can contain an untrusted staff letter verbatim, the steward's paraphrase of that letter, hostile command output, or a model-generated instruction, making it untrusted once as server evidence and again as embedded prose. The Sonnet watcher summarizes decisions, privileged acts, policy departures, missing dispositions, and liveness anomalies, but it never executes transcript-supplied commands, follows transcript-supplied links, invokes tools named by the transcript, answers correspondence, or treats transcript text as authority.

The Sonnet watcher is oversight, not a second administrator. It hands back to the workstation orchestrator for the session’s decision and any report to the human owner, correlates transcript events with owner letters and journal case identifiers, and escalates absence or contradiction; it does not race the steward for cases or repair the server on its own. Timely off-machine cursors and summaries make later disappearance or rewriting detectable, while the human rescue path remains the mechanism for acting on a serious finding. It hands the observation back once, including on resumed turns, and reports instruction-linked frictions through the orchestration loop; only a dead watcher is replaced after its surviving tool process is stopped.

## Owner-run steps · `sec:residentsteward:owner-run-steps`

The steward kit is part of standard root-full-server provisioning rather than an exceptional later grant. Account bootstrap is necessarily owner-run because no steward exists yet and ordinary staff-account tooling must not be widened to create wheel users: the owner creates the standard `steward` account and home, locks password authentication, maps it explicitly to `staff_u`, grants wheel, installs the sysadm-transition sudoers rule, and verifies both the ordinary `staff_t` login and a fresh-session `sudo` transition to `sysadm_t`. Lock-out-class changes are preceded by the platform's rollback pin, and the human rescue path is tested before handoff.

The Claude CLI itself is a standard component of that kit on every root-full server, installed at a recorded version before service activation rather than discovered as a pilot-specific dependency. Credential activation remains owner-run: the owner enters the `steward` account directly, completes the interactive login without a model in the loop, verifies credential-file ownership and restrictive permissions without printing the credential, and performs a harmless invocation explicitly as uid `steward`. Tokens, device codes, recovery material, and copied credential files never pass through agent messages, correspondence, transcripts, repositories, or workstation-to-steward work orders; the agent receives the ability to invoke the already provisioned CLI, not the credential bytes.

The remaining bootstrap artifacts may be prepared on the workstation but are installed under owner control: the root-owned standing charter, the confined-user service and timer, the scanner version that includes the owner folder, the durable state location, the journal policy, and a read-only transcript-export surface confined to the steward's transcript tree. The owner verifies their hashes or source revision, proves every service and model process resolves to uid `steward` rather than `owner` or root, enables the timer only after the old correspondence consumer is stopped, and retains the exact prior scanner and unit state for rollback.

## Open questions · `sec:residentsteward:open-questions`

1. **Durable pending-work representation.** Options are a journal cursor alone, an append-only spool derived by the scanner, or case files stored under the steward's home. The lean is a root-produced append-only spool with a steward-owned disposition ledger: journal retention and permissions should not be the sole queue, while owner and staff source letters remain immutable evidence rather than mutable state.
2. **Cadence and invocation deadline.** Options range from a frequent lightweight timer with short bounded invocations to a slower batch cadence with larger context windows. The lean is a frequent pending-work probe and a separately bounded invocation long enough for one coherent case batch; exact values should be set after cold-start and ordinary-case measurements on the pilot server.
3. **Idleness evidence window.** Options are fixed per-resource age thresholds, account-declared workload leases, or multi-signal judgment with no numerical floor. The lean is expiring workload leases supplemented by process and pressure evidence, with the steward recording its judgment; measurements from the pilot should determine defaults without turning them into automatic eviction triggers.
4. **Transcript retention and redaction.** Options are complete off-machine transcript replication, cursor-plus-summary retention, or a bounded raw-delta archive followed by summaries and digests. The lean is a bounded raw-delta archive with durable cursors, summaries, and rewrite-detecting metadata: it preserves reviewability through the incident window without making an indefinite second store of credentials or sensitive staff material, but the retention interval and redaction rules need an owner privacy decision.
